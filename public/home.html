How it works

Four steps take a drain from blocked to cleared, and you are told when it is done.

  1. Report

    Say how the drain is (clear, partly blocked or fully blocked) and where it is. Use your location or pick a landmark.

  2. Rank

    DrainWatch scores each drain using the blockage, the rain forecast, the flood risk and how many people are nearby.

  3. Clear

    The assembly assigns the top drains to crews. Crews get directions and add a photo when the drain is cleared.

  4. Confirm

    You get a message asking if the drain is really clear. If you say it is still blocked, the crew is sent back.

Why it helps

Drains are usually cleared on a fixed schedule or after a complaint. DrainWatch adds the missing information: which drain matters most right now.

  • It watches the rain

    The 72-hour forecast shows how many drains crews can reach before the heaviest rain, and which ones are at risk.

  • It listens to neighbours

    When two or more people report the same drain, it moves up the list. This also makes false reports harder.

  • It checks the work

    A cleared drain is only final when a resident confirms it, or two days pass with no complaint.

  • It finds quiet areas

    High-risk drains with no recent report are flagged for a visit, so areas that report less are not forgotten.
